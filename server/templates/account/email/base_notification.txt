Overrides allauth's own base_notification.txt — the frame every after-the-fact
notification mail extends (password_changed, email_changed, password_set,
email_deleted), switched on by ACCOUNT_EMAIL_NOTIFICATIONS.

Only the closing advice is changed. Stock says "If you do not recognize this
change then please take proper security precautions immediately", which names
no control and no address: it tells an alarmed reader that something is wrong
and leaves them to work out what to do about it. Same objection as the stock
wording in account_already_exists_message.txt, and the same fix — name the
thing the reader can actually do.

**What they can do is write to us, and specifically not "reset your
password".** These mails exist for the takeover case, and the mail that matters
most, `email_changed`, is sent to the address being moved *away from* — so by
the time it arrives, reset codes are already going to the attacker's address.
Advising a reset would be advising the one step that no longer works, on the
one occasion the reader most needs to be told something true. So the text says
what a reset can and cannot do, and points at a human.

**Stock's IP and user-agent lines are dropped; only the timestamp is kept.**
Sending someone their own IP is normal practice, so the omission is deliberate
rather than an oversight:

- **It buys us nothing we don't already have.** A report names the account, and
  this mail names the time — which locates the request in Caddy's access log
  without the reader quoting anything back. The log holds the IP and the
  user-agent for 30 days either way (PRIVACY.md §2), so the mail was echoing a
  value we can look up ourselves.
- **It is close to noise for the reader,** who is the one meant to act on it. A
  raw IPv4 and a raw UA string do not tell most people whether a session was
  theirs, and an unfamiliar-looking address that is really their own ISP
  misleads in the alarming direction. The time is the part anyone can judge.
- **It invites personal data into the support inbox.** "Include the details
  below" with an IP in them means abuse reports arrive at mailbox.org carrying
  data we otherwise keep only in a log we have promised to delete in 30 days.

If a real incident ever needs more than the timestamp, it is in the access log
for a month — which is the right place for it, not in a mail we cannot unsend.

English-only for the same reason as the other account mails (L10N.md); the
blocktranslate tags are kept so the strings stay extractable.
{% endcomment %}
